How the scores work
  • Recovery (0 to 100). Each input is compared with its own trailing 28-day mean and spread (at least 10 days of history). Weights: HRV 40% (log scale), resting HR 20% (lower is better), heart-rate braking 15%, sleep hours 15%, wrist temperature 10% (only a swing beyond 1 SD counts against you). Missing inputs are dropped and the remaining weights rescale. The weighted z-score is passed through the normal curve, so 50 is your normal and 84 is a weighted z-score of +1. Bands: under 34 low, 34 to 66 moderate, 67 and above high.
  • Sleep (0 to 100). 60% duration against need, 20% deep sleep share (full marks at 13% of the night), 20% REM share (full marks at 20%). Need is the value you set on the Sleep tab, plus 0.5 h after a day whose load ran 30% above your chronic load. Apple stage splits are estimates.
  • Exertion (0 to 100). Day load is zone 1 minutes x1, zone 2 x2, zone 3 x3, zone 4 x4, zone 5 x5 (Edwards method). Score = 100 x (1 - exp(-load / k)), where k is 1.5 times your median day, so a typical day scores about 49.
  • Target band. Chronic load (28-day exponential average) times 0.4 to 0.8 when recovery is low, 0.8 to 1.2 when moderate, 1.1 to 1.6 when high. These multipliers are a judgement, not a fitted result.
  • Load ratio. Acute (7-day) over chronic (28-day) exponential averages. 0.8 to 1.3 is the usual working band.
  • Call of the day. Ease off if recovery is under 34 or the ratio entering the day is above 1.5. Push if recovery is 67 or above and the ratio is 1.3 or lower. Otherwise hold steady. A rule of thumb, not medical advice.
  • Not available. Respiratory rate, stress, bed and wake times, SpO2 and nutrition are not in the export, so they cannot feed a score. Scores before 28 days of history are less stable.
